Title: WHY SINGLE-CONNECTION DOWNLOADS ARE OFTEN SLOW
Author & Architect: Gaurav Patil (https://gauravpatil.site)

================================================================================

Have you ever wondered why a file downloads at only 2 MB/s in your web browser,
even though your internet plan provides 100 MB/s?

The reason comes down to how standard web servers work.

When your browser downloads a file in a single connection, the server's network
scheduler often limits the speed of that specific connection to prevent any one
user from overloading their network. Furthermore, network latency—the physical
distance between your computer and the server—causes delays in acknowledging
received data packets (known as the TCP bandwidth-delay product).

If a single packet gets lost, the connection artificially slows down to prevent
congestion, even if your Wi-Fi is capable of much higher speeds.

Downex solves this by breaking the artificial speed ceiling. Instead of asking
the server for the file through one narrow pipe, it asks for the file through
multiple parallel pipes at the same time.
