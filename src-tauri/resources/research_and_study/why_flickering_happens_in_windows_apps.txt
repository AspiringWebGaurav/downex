Title: WHY FLICKERING HAPPENS IN WINDOWS APPS
Author & Architect: Gaurav Patil (https://gauravpatil.site)

Have you ever seen a window or progress bar in Windows that flickers, strobes,
or flashes black-and-white every time it updates?

To understand why this happens, you have to look at the Windows GDI (Graphics
Device Interface) pipeline:
1. When a window wants to update, Windows sends an erase message (WM_ERASEBKGND).
2. By default, Windows erases the entire window canvas with a blank background.
3. A split second later, the app draws its text and buttons on top.
4. If this cycle happens 40 or 60 times a second, your eyes see the rapid back-
   and-forth between the blank background and the drawn text. This is flickering!

Furthermore, if the app draws its elements directly to the screen one by one,
the monitor often refreshes halfway through drawing, causing visual tearing.

Downex eliminates 100% of this flicker through two proven techniques:
- It intercepts WM_ERASEBKGND and halts default background erasure completely.
- It uses Double-Buffering: the entire frame is drawn in RAM off-screen, and then
  flashed to the monitor in one single atomic blit. Zero flicker, guaranteed.
